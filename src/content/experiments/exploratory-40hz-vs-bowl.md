## Label: exploratory / methods note

No human subjects. This is a **design hygiene** writeup for the hub.

## The conflation problem

Marketing copy sometimes borrows “40 Hz” language from vibroacoustic therapy (VAT) literature and pastes it onto crystal-bowl sound baths. Kantor et al. 2022 (BMJ Open scoping review) studied **device-delivered low-frequency vibration**, often 40 Hz, sessions 20–45 minutes — and still concluded evidence is too sparse for firm pain claims.

## Our rule on this site

| Modality | Typical stimulus | Evidence posture here |
|----------|------------------|------------------------|
| VAT | Mechanical low-frequency vibration ± music | Weak / sparse (Kantor) |
| Singing bowls | Airborne acoustic + ritual/group context | Weak (Cai; Goldsby) |
| Music therapy | Credentialed clinical relationship | Mixed→stronger for some short-term adjuncts |

## Takeaway

Different mechanisms, different delivery, different evidence files. Borrowing VAT’s frequency talk to sell bowls fails our FTC-safe bar.
