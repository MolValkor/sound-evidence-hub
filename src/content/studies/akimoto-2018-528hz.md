## Design

Crossover, **n = 9** healthy adults (1 man, 8 women, ages 26–37). Same soothing piano music, 5 minutes, 528 Hz-tuned versus 440 Hz-tuned on separate days starting 2 PM; participants not told which track. Open PDF via SCIRP (CC BY 4.0).

## Reported signals (short-term lab)

- Salivary cortisol: significant drop after 528 Hz at 30 min; 440 Hz not significant
- Oxytocin: significant rise immediately after 528 Hz; 440 Hz increase not significant
- Chromogranin A: no significant change either condition
- Autonomic / POMS: LF/HF fell after both; tension-anxiety and total mood disturbance fell significantly after 528 Hz only
- Authors note tiny sample and very short exposure

## Honest framing

**Not singing bowls** — this is a piano tuning comparison often mis-cited in frequency-medicine marketing. Authors state various effects ascribed to solfeggio “**have [no] scientific basis**,” then present this pilot. Cite as exploratory endocrine/mood signals after 5 minutes of music, **not** as proof of solfeggio medicine. Evidence strength: **weak**.
